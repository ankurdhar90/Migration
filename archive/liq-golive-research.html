LIQ migration · research for SteerCo

How others went live

I looked at published migrations to Snowflake and other cloud platforms from Teradata, Oracle, SQL Server, Hadoop and PostgreSQL, including several banks, plus vendor and cloud-provider guidance and established legacy-replacement patterns. The common thread is clear: the programmes that went well switched in slices or with an easy way back, and they ran old and new side by side long enough to cover real business cycles.

1

Big bang is the documented failure mode. TSB's 2018 review found its big-bang approach "played a key part in the IT failure", and the board never seriously weighed the alternatives.

2

Parallel runs last weeks to months, not days. Published figures range from a few days in Snowflake's 2020 Teradata guide, through 30–90 days in practitioner guides, to at least 6 months at a UK bank.

3

Exit criteria are numeric and tied to business cycles. One example: two consecutive billing cycles under 0.01% variance before cutover.

4

A temporary layer that feeds legacy consumers is normal. Thoughtworks calls it Legacy Mimic and says keeping the existing aggregator fed this way is "the more common approach".

The strategies people use

Every approach found sits somewhere on two dials: how much switches at once, and how long old and new run side by side. Positions are my judgement from the sources.

The patterns behind the cases

Shadow mode

The new path runs on real inputs but can't act. Compare normalised outputs, sort each mismatch into timing, representation, semantic, data quality or defect, and aim for zero unexplained differences.

dev.to critical data path · Fowler: Dark Launching

Parallel run with reconciliation

Both systems produce the real output. Automated end-to-end reconciliation runs every day, and exit criteria are a variance threshold sustained over a set period.

Deloitte · Milemarker · Version 1 · CommBank

Offload, then full migration

Move a use case's data and consumers first while the old pipelines still feed it. Then move the pipelines and retire the legacy tables.

Google BigQuery migration guide

Steel thread

Take the hardest real slice all the way into production first. Unlike a proof of concept, it operationalises the result.

CommBank · Snowflake guides: start high-impact, low-complexity

Legacy Mimic

The new system serves or feeds legacy in its existing shape, so the old side doesn't know anything changed. It's transitional, so plan its removal from the start.

Thoughtworks Patterns of Legacy Displacement · bridging tables

Consumer by consumer

Repoint each report, app or product one at a time and validate each connection. Outbound feeds go last and get row-by-row checks.

7Rivers · Welcome to the Jungle

Blue/green swap

Build and test in a separate schema or database, then swap atomically. If anything fails, you simply don't switch.

dbt community · Snowflake SWAP WITH

Rollback by design

Defined rollback checkpoints, a named decision-maker, a known point of no return, and a rollback rehearsed with its time measured.

AWS Prescriptive Guidance · cutover worksheet

Soak, then delete

Keep legacy as a fallback, often read-only, through a clean period. Delete only after it, and keep a cold backup.

Snowflake guides · NAB · Welcome to the Jungle

Correction to the go-live page. Microsoft's documentation says that when a table moves with ALTER SCHEMA TRANSFER, "all permissions associated with the securable are dropped", and references in triggers and modules aren't updated. The transfer also locks the whole schema, not just the table. So grants have to be reapplied to both tables, and the swap needs a window where nothing else reads that schema. The go-live page said permissions stay with the old table, which is wrong.

The best composite for LIQ

Taking the strongest elements from the evidence, the LIQ go-live would look like this. It keeps the three states and adds the two things the best programmes had that we don't yet: a steel thread and numeric exit criteria.

  1. Formalise the shadowDaily automated value-level reconciliation of SnowIn against tier-1 at all four levels, with breaks classified and a numeric exit gate.
  2. Steel threadOne subject area (Deposits, if its joins allow) goes through in and out, reconciliation and rollback on a prod copy.
  3. In and out, all of tier-1The alias switch, which is a Legacy Mimic. Run it live across a month-end and a quarter-end.
  4. Go-live by swapBlue/green if the batch window allows, a rename if not. Back up history first and reapply grants.
  5. Soak, then decommissionLegacy stays fed until a clean period passes, with a cold backup kept after that.

An option for later phases: Thoughtworks' Divert the Flow pattern moves the critical aggregator, here the liquidity reporting itself, to the new platform early. Keeping it on legacy behind a mimic is common, but it couples you to legacy formats and timings for longer. That's worth raising when SteerCo plans the phases after tier-1.

How far to trust this

  • Most case studies are vendor-published and self-reported. Few give cutover steps or rollback detail.
  • No public case matched our exact shape, with Snowflake output landed back into SQL Server for unchanged downstream consumers. The closest are the Legacy Mimic and bridging-table patterns.
  • Durations and thresholds come from the sources cited. Where the LIQ ones are marked "suggested", that's my recommendation.
